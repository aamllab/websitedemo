A Landscape Transformed

The stretch of Kwun Tong Road near Kai Yip Estate is more than just a thoroughfare; it is a physical timeline of Hong Kong's 20th-century history. From the roar of Royal Air Force engines in the 1920s to the quiet study of art students today, this land has served the colony's defence, sheltered refugees, and now houses thousands of citizens.

The Kwun Tong Road Timeline

75 Years of Rapid Change

Start

1927: RAF Base Established

The Royal Air Force (RAF) officially forms its station at the Eastern End of the Kai Tak Aerodrome, on land reclaimed from Kowloon Bay.

🚩 The Colonial Era Begins
'34

1934: Historic Buildings Rise

Construction of the Officers' Quarters Compound and Mess (now HKBU Campus) on the hillside. Built in Colonial Neo-Classical style.

War

1941–1945: Japanese Occupation

The base is seized and used by the Japanese Military during WWII. A dark period of conflict and trauma for the area.

✈️

Post-1950s: The Height Constraint

Strict height restrictions enforced across Kowloon to allow safe flight paths. Kwun Tong development is kept low-rise.

🔄

1978-79: Closure & Crisis

The RAF Base closes. The HQ building is immediately repurposed as the Kai Tak Vietnamese Refugee Camp.

🏠

1981: Kai Yip Estate Opens

The main RAF maintenance area is transformed into public housing, providing homes for thousands.

Now

1998–Present: Heritage & High-Rises

Airport closes (1998). Height limits lifted. Historic buildings preserved as HKBU Academy of Visual Arts (2001).
